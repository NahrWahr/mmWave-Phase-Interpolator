\section{Theoretical Analysis}
\label{sec:analysis}

\subsection{Phase Interpolation Mechanics}

The CMPI synthesises an arbitrary phase $\phi_\mathrm{interp}$ between two
boundary phasors $\vec{V}_1$ and $\vec{V}_2$ by weighting their tail currents:
\begin{equation}
    \vec{V}_\mathrm{out} = W_1 \vec{V}_1 + W_2 \vec{V}_2,
    \label{eq:interp}
\end{equation}
where $W_1$ and $W_2$ are normalised weights summing to unity.
Fig.~\ref{fig:phasor} illustrates the construction for $\vec{V}_1$ at
$120^\circ$ and $\vec{V}_2$ at $150^\circ$, producing a resultant at
$140^\circ$.

\subsection{Phase Noise Through the Frequency Doubler}
\label{sec:pn}

For a generic $\times N$ frequency multiplier, phase noise transforms as
\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{out}(f_\Delta)
    = \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{in}(f_\Delta)
    + 20\log_{10}(N)
    + \mathcal{L}_\mathrm{add}(f_\Delta),
    \label{eq:pn_mult}
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{add}$ is the additive noise contribution of the
doubler itself. For $N=2$ the irreducible degradation is
\begin{equation}
    \Delta\mathcal{L} = 20\log_{10}(2) \approx 6\,\mathrm{dB}.
    \label{eq:6dB}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Absolute Jitter Preservation}

Time jitter $\sigma_t$ relates to integrated phase variance $\sigma_\phi^2$
and carrier frequency $f_c$ by
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_t = \frac{\sigma_\phi}{2\pi f_c}.
    \label{eq:jitter}
\end{equation}
After the $\times 2$ doubler the phase variance scales as $N^2\sigma_\phi^2$
while the frequency becomes $Nf_c$, yielding
\begin{equation}
    \sigma_{t,\mathrm{out}}
    = \frac{N\,\sigma_{\phi,\mathrm{in}}}{2\pi (N f_\mathrm{in})}
    = \sigma_{t,\mathrm{in}}.
    \label{eq:jitter_preserved}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:jitter_preserved} shows that an ideal frequency doubler
preserves absolute jitter: the \SI{6}{\decibel} phase noise degradation is
exactly cancelled by the factor-of-two increase in carrier frequency.

\subsection{Injection Locking Dynamics}

Beyond the free-running doubler noise floor, injection locking confines the
output phase noise to the $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{in}+6\,\mathrm{dB}$ bound
within the locking bandwidth $f_L$. Outside $f_L$ the output reverts to the
free-running profile, as illustrated in Fig.~\ref{fig:pn}.
